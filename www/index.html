    SPLITS PROGRESSIONKurz method — isometric emphasis

    ⚠️ Before You Begin

    The hips and knee joints must be able to assume the required positions. Take the flexibility potential tests first. If your hip structure prevents full splits, work to your own safe maximum — never force. Do a warm-up first; do splits work after training, never before.

    FRONT SPLIT — PROGRESSION ORDER

    Work through these in order. Each builds the foundation for the next. If you feel pain in the knee or hip joint, stop and regress one step.

    SIDE SPLIT

    Side Split Cautions

    Weak adductors are the main obstacle. If the sides of your hips hurt when you kick or open your legs sideways, you are not tilting the pelvis enough. Do not progress to wider stances until you are comfortable at the current stage.

    BALANCING

    Balance the Work

    Side split work stresses adductors and internal rotators. To keep the hips healthy, alternate with the outer thigh and external rotator stretches below. Unbalanced hip flexibility is a known cause of low back pain.

    HANDSTAND TRAINING3–5×/week · 10–20 min

    How to Use This Tab

    A handstand is a skill, not a lift — frequency beats volume. Practise 3–5×/week in short, fresh sessions, ideally before your main workout and never after heavy pressing. Always run the wrist & shoulder prep first. Stop a set the moment your line breaks: quality holds build the skill, grinding builds wrist pain.

    SESSION TEMPLATE

    1. Wrist & shoulder prep — 4–6 min (every session)
    2. Wall drills — 5–8 min (chest-to-wall focus)
    3. Balance drills — 5–8 min (freestanding attempts)
    4. Strength — 5–8 min (2×/week only)
    5. Wrist cool-down — 2 min

    WEEKLY FREQUENCY

    MONFull session — prep → wall → balance → strength
    TUEShort — prep → wall drills only (5 min)
    WEDRest from handstands (wrists recover)
    THUFull session — prep → wall → balance → strength
    FRIShort — prep → balance attempts only (5 min)
    SATFull session — prep → wall → balance → skill links
    SUNRest — wrists and shoulders off load

    1. WRIST & SHOULDER PREP

    Non-negotiable before every handstand session. Wrists adapt far more slowly than shoulders — never train on cold wrists, and back off at the first sign of sharp wrist pain.

    2. WALL DRILLS

    Chest-to-wall is the money drill — it forces a straight line, open shoulders and hollow ribs. Back-to-wall is easier and teaches the kick-up; use it as a stepping stone, not a destination.

    3. BALANCE DRILLS

    Balance is finger pressure, not arm strength. Elbows locked, push the floor away, and correct with the fingertips before the palm ever moves.

    4. STRENGTH & CONDITIONING

    Twice a week is plenty. Strength supports the skill — it does not replace practice time on the wall.

    5. CAPOEIRA INTEGRATION

    Where the handstand pays off in the roda: bananeira, queda de rins, aú sem mão and macaco entries.

    COMMON FAULTS

    ⚠ Fix These First

    Banana back — ribs flared, lower back arched. Tuck the pelvis, squeeze glutes, pull ribs down.

    Bent elbows — you are not pushing the floor away. Lock out and shrug the shoulders up to the ears.

    Staring at the floor — look at your hands, or just between them. Head neutral, ears between the arms.

    Hands too far from the wall — walk in until chest and thighs actually touch. Only then does chest-to-wall work.

    Kicking too hard — you will overbalance and never learn the correction. Kick with just enough force to reach the wall.

    Skipping wrist prep — wrist pain ends handstand training faster than anything else.
